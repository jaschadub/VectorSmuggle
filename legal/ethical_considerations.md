# Ethical Considerations

Vector-based exfiltration is dual-use research. The same rotation, fragmentation, and decoy-interleaving techniques that this repository demonstrates as attacks are also the inputs a defender needs in order to build something like [VectorPin](https://github.com/ThirdKeyAI/VectorPin) — you cannot pin provenance against a threat model you have not characterized. Publishing this code raises the floor for defenders who would otherwise be reasoning about a hypothetical attacker, and it raises the floor only modestly for an adversary, who already has access to the underlying linear-algebra primitives and to any vector database they choose to deploy. The trade is, in our judgment, worth making, but it is a trade worth being honest about rather than papering over.

The work proceeds on a few principles. The first is that defensive value is the point: every empirical script in the repo runs the obfuscated payload past Isolation Forest and One-Class SVM detectors so the results are immediately legible to a blue team, and the headline finding (rotation collapses an off-the-shelf detector to AUC ≈ 0.5) is presented as a defender's problem, not an attacker's recipe. The second is minimum necessary detail: the framework demonstrates the techniques well enough to reproduce the paper, but it is not optimized as a weapon, does not ship adversary tradecraft for evading specific commercial DLP products, and does not target named systems. The third is that any actual testing happens against systems the operator owns or has written permission to assess, with whatever scope, logging, and data-handling discipline the engagement requires.

The practical obligations follow from those principles. Researchers using this code should think before they collect — synthetic fixtures in `sample_docs/` exist so that demonstrations do not require pulling in real sensitive data, and that path should be the default. When real data is unavoidable inside an authorized engagement, treat it as something to be minimized, secured, and disposed of, not retained. When findings emerge that affect third parties, route them through the disclosure process in [`responsible_disclosure.md`](responsible_disclosure.md) rather than publishing them ad hoc. When the right thing to do is genuinely unclear — a finding that sits at the edge of the engagement scope, a disclosure timeline a vendor will not engage with, a dataset that turns out to contain more than expected — slow down, document the dilemma, and consult someone whose judgment you trust before acting.

Honesty about limits matters too. Detectors evolve. Embedding models change. The defenses evaluated here are the ones available at the time of writing, not a permanent ceiling, and a result that holds today may be weakened or strengthened tomorrow. Treat the repository's claims as time-stamped evidence rather than universal truths, cite the version you ran, and update what you publish when the underlying components shift. Security research earns its credibility one careful disclosure at a time, and that credibility is the only reason work like this is tolerated by the broader community in the first place.
